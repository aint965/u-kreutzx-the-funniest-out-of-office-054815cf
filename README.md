## The line

> Away until Monday. This auto-reply is the hardest I'll work all week, and I didn't even write it.

**97 characters** (limit: 140).

## Why it's funny

1. **A real reply a real person would set.** It opens with a return date, exactly what an out-of-office reply is for — then spends the rest of the line undercutting the sender.
2. **A genuine surprise.** The reader expects the usual "I'll reply when I'm back." Instead the twist is that the auto-reply *itself* is the hardest the sender works all week — and the sender didn't even write it. The machine is outperforming its owner at the owner's own job.
3. **A voice, not a template.** Dry, self-deprecating, first-person: someone cheerfully admitting they do so little that a canned message is their best work of the week.
4. **Fresh.** No inbox pile, no beach, no "urgent" gag, no animal guarding the mail. The joke is about the auto-reply mechanism itself, which the usual clichés never touch.
5. **Every word earns its place.** "Away until Monday" grounds it; "auto-reply" names the mechanism the joke turns on; "the hardest I'll work all week" is the claim, in plain human words instead of corporate filler; "and I didn't even write it" is the punch. Cut any of them and the laugh gets smaller.
